Academic Record · Istanbul University · 2015
Master's Thesis · Islamic Law & Finance

An Investigation on the Economical Life
of the Holy Prophet

Halil Ibrahim Ciger·Istanbul University·2015
Preface

This thesis examines the economic dimensions of the Prophet Muhammad's life through a rigorous interdisciplinary lens, drawing on classical Islamic sources and contemporary economic analysis.

The study illuminates neglected dimensions of prophetic biography using methodologies from both Islamic jurisprudence and modern economic science, revealing a coherent and principled economic framework.

Chapter I · Pre-Prophetic Period

Examines trade practices in 6th-century Arabia, the Prophet's early commercial activities as merchant and trade agent for Khadijah, partnership arrangements (mudaraba), and the ethical principles he established before his prophethood.

Chapter II · The Medina Market

Analyses the construction of the first Islamic market in Medina, the prohibition of monopolistic and deceptive practices, introduction of standardised weights and measures, and foundational principles of Islamic economic justice.

Chapter III · Fiscal Policy

Covers zakat administration as a redistributive institution, rules governing spoils of war and their equitable distribution, land taxation (kharaj), and the Prophet's sophisticated approach to public finance.

Chapter IV · Legacy & Modern Relevance

Discusses the enduring influence of prophetic economic principles on Islamic finance, waqf institutions, profit-sharing structures, and contemporary Muslim-majority economies. Draws a line from 7th-century Medina to modern instruments such as sukuk and participation banking.

Conclusion

The Prophet's economic model constitutes a coherent system balancing spiritual imperatives with pragmatic governance — an emphasis on transparency, prohibition of exploitation, and communal solidarity that remains relevant for Islamic economics today.

"A principled economic model rooted in justice, transparency, and communal solidarity."
Istanbul University · Department of Basic Islamic Sciences
Academic Record · Uludag University · 2010
Bachelor's Thesis · Economics

Economic Analysis of
Participation Banks

Halil Ibrahim Ciger·Uludag University·2010
Overview

This thesis examines participation banking as an alternative financial model grounded in Islamic economic principles, analysing its structure, operations, and performance within the Turkish banking sector.

The study investigates the theoretical foundations of interest-free finance, the distinction between conventional and participation banking, and the practical implementation of profit-and-loss sharing mechanisms in modern banking.

Chapter I · Islamic Finance Principles

Covers the Quranic prohibition of riba (interest), the concept of halal earnings, and the foundational principles that distinguish participation banking from conventional finance. Explores mudaraba (profit-sharing) and musharaka (joint venture) as core instruments.

Chapter II · Historical Development

Traces the emergence of Islamic banking from the 1960s onward, the establishment of participation banks in Turkey (formerly known as special finance houses), and their legal and regulatory evolution under Turkish banking law.

Chapter III · Structural Analysis

Examines fund collection methods (current accounts, participation accounts), fund allocation methods (production support, finance leasing, profit-loss partnership), and how participation banks manage liquidity without interest-based instruments.

Chapter IV · Performance Evaluation

Analyses balance sheet structures, profitability ratios, asset quality, and capital adequacy of Turkish participation banks compared to conventional counterparts. Evaluates growth trends, market share, and systemic integration.

Chapter V · Challenges & Opportunities

Identifies key challenges including lack of standardisation, limited product diversity, public awareness deficits, and regulatory asymmetry. Highlights growth opportunities in ethical finance, sukuk markets, and international Islamic finance integration.

Conclusion

Participation banks represent a viable and principled alternative to conventional finance, offering a model that aligns economic activity with ethical and spiritual values. Their continued growth requires regulatory support, product innovation, and greater public understanding of interest-free finance principles.

"A financial model that aligns economic activity with ethical principles — interest-free, transparent, and community-centred."
Uludag University · Faculty of Economics · Department of Economics
